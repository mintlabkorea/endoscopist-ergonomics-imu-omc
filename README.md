## Abstract
Objective measurements of ergonomic burden during gastrointestinal endoscopy and evidence supporting workstation recommendations remain limited. We characterized region-specific postural exposure during routinescreening esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGD) and evaluated how bed and monitor height affected posture during simulated EGD. Nine endoscopists wore inertial measurement units during routine screening EGD, and 29procedures were analyzed. Five of these endoscopists also completed 75 simulated EGD trials under controlled bed- and monitor-height conditions using optical motion capture. Joint kinematics were converted into Rapid Upper Limb Assessment (RULA) scores. During routine EGD, the mean overall RULA score was 5.60 (SD 0.30), and scores fell in the moderate or high categories for 94.4% of procedure time. The wrist had the highest normalized joint-level RULA score (71.7%, SD 12.4%), while neck scores remained in the moderate category for97.3% of procedure time. In simulated EGD, raising the bed toward elbow height reduced the normalized lower-arm RULA score by 6.6 percentage points per level (P = 0.019). Raising the monitor increased neck extension by5.1° per level (P = 0.002), without a corresponding significant trend in neck RULA scores. Continuous motion measurements can complement standardized ergonomic scores by revealing posture changes that those scores may not capture.
